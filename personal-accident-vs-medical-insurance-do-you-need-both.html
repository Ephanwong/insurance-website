Personal Accident vs Medical Insurance: Do You Really Need Both?

Wong Ket Pong · July 2026 · 6 min read

"I already have personal accident insurance, so I don't need medical insurance" is one of the most common — and most costly — misunderstandings I hear from clients. These two types of cover sound similar and are often sold together, but they protect against very different things.

What Personal Accident (PA) insurance covers

PA insurance pays out when an accident — not illness — causes injury, disability, or death. It typically covers:

  • A lump sum for accidental death or permanent disability
  • Reimbursement of medical expenses arising specifically from the accident
  • Weekly income replacement during recovery, on some plans

The key word is accident. A fall, a road accident, a workplace injury — all qualify. A heart attack, cancer, or a burst appendix does not, no matter how serious.

What Medical insurance covers

Medical insurance is built around illness and hospitalisation in general — not just accidents. It typically covers:

  • Hospital room and board during admission
  • Surgery and specialist consultation fees
  • Treatment for illnesses like infections, organ conditions, or cancer
  • Outpatient and pre/post-hospitalisation care, depending on the plan

The gap if you only have one

If you only have PA cover and you're diagnosed with a serious illness that has nothing to do with an accident, your PA policy simply won't respond — you'd be paying hospital bills entirely out of pocket.

If you only have medical cover and you suffer a disabling accident, some medical plans may cover the hospital treatment, but won't provide the lump-sum payout for permanent disability or loss of income that a PA plan is specifically designed for.

Think of it this way: medical insurance protects against the cost of getting sick or injured. Personal accident insurance protects against the financial impact of losing a limb, your ability to work, or your life — as a direct result of an accident.

Who should consider having both

Both types of cover are relatively affordable on their own, which is why many advisors — myself included — recommend them as a pair rather than a choice between one or the other, especially for:

  • Working adults who are the main income earner in the household
  • Parents of young children
  • Anyone in a manual, driving, or higher-risk occupation
