5 Risks Every Sabah Homeowner Should Insure Against (Not Just Fire)

Wong Ket Pong · July 2026 · 6 min read

If you own a home in Sabah, chances are the only insurance you have is the fire policy your bank required when you took out your mortgage. It's often treated as a box to tick rather than real protection — and that's a problem, because fire is actually one of the less common risks your home faces.

Here are five risks that catch homeowners off guard, and what kind of cover actually protects against them.

1. Flood damage

Sabah's monsoon season brings flash floods to many residential areas, especially homes near rivers, drains, or low-lying land. Here's the part most people don't realise: a standard fire policy in Malaysia does not automatically include flood cover. It has to be added as an extension.

If your home has ever experienced pooling water during heavy rain, or you live in an area prone to flash floods, this extension is worth checking today — not after the next storm.

2. Burst pipes and water damage

Water damage from burst pipes, overflowing tanks, or leaking roofs is one of the most common claims for homeowners — and one of the most expensive to repair, since it often damages flooring, wiring, and furniture along with the structure itself.

3. Theft and burglary

Landed properties and homes outside gated communities are more exposed to break-ins. A proper home policy covers not just forced-entry theft, but often the cost of repairing damaged doors, windows, or grilles used to gain entry.

4. Landslide and land subsidence

Given Sabah's hilly terrain, homes built on slopes or reclaimed land face a real risk of landslide or ground subsidence — something many homeowners don't think about until retaining walls crack or the ground shifts. This is a separate peril from fire and needs to be specifically covered.

5. Liability to other people

If a tree in your compound falls onto your neighbour's car, or a visitor slips and gets injured on your property, you could be held legally responsible for the cost. Public liability cover protects you from these claims, which can be surprisingly large.

A good rule of thumb: insure your home for what it would cost to rebuild it from scratch — not what it would sell for on the market. Land value doesn't need to be insured; only the structure does.

How much coverage do you actually need?

This is where many homeowners get it wrong in both directions — under-insuring (which can reduce your payout under the "average clause" if you ever claim) or over-insuring and paying more than necessary. The right sum insured depends on your home's size, construction type, and local rebuilding costs.

If you're not sure whether your current policy actually covers flood, water damage, or liability — or if you've never reviewed your sum insured — it's worth a five-minute conversation to check.
